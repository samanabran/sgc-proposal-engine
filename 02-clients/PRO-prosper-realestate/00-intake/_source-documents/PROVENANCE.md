## Metadata caveat — read before treating the internal date as proof

`docx.core_properties` on the original file: `author: "python-docx"`,
`last_modified_by: "Dian Sajulga"`, `created`/`modified`:
`2026-07-21 05:31:00+00:00`, `comments: "generated by python-docx"`.

The `author` field naming the generating **library**, not a person or
"Microsoft Word," means this file was produced or reprocessed through a
script at some point, not authored end-to-end natively in Word. That
does not cast doubt on the document's content or Dian Sajulga's actual
authorship (`last_modified_by` does name her, and the content is
internally consistent — office address, requirement style, and framing
all match the client's known profile).

**Two explanations for the `author: python-docx` field, both plausible,
neither confirmed** (corrected 2026-08-06 — the entry previously read as
if this were the more suspicious of the two without saying so):

1. **The file-conversion pipeline that produced the copy shared with this
   repo** — a Word document opened and re-saved, or converted, by any
   automated tool built on the `python-docx` library (a common step when
   preparing a file for sharing/attaching) would overwrite the `author`
   field this way regardless of who wrote the content. This is the more
   likely explanation given no other signal points to the client's own
   process — most requirements documents pass through at least one such
   conversion between drafting and reaching a third party.
2. The client's own drafting process used a script-based tool rather than
   Word directly — less likely (the content and formatting read as
   native Word/Google Docs output), but not ruled out.

**This does not reduce the document's grade.** The metadata caveat means
the internal timestamp (07/21/2026) is not independently provable as the
true authorship date — it says nothing about whether Dian Sajulga wrote
the content. Absent evidence pointing the other way, explanation 1 above
is treated as the working assumption, and this remains **the strongest
client-authored artifact in Prosper's corpus** until the receipt channel
itself is established (see "How it reached this repo" above) — a
question about custody, not about authorship or content.
